Research · November 2026 Test-time learning / Reasoning

Introduction

AI agents can plan, write code, use tools, and carry out increasingly complex tasks. Their progress also depends on the structures that organize the work: the question they choose to ask, the abstractions they use, the alternatives they consider, and the evidence they seek. A useful next step may be to revise one of those structures.

Consider a system observed through a camera. Every recorded frame may fit an equilibrium model, even though a circulating current exists between frames. More accurate fitting at the same sampling interval will preserve the ambiguity. Progress begins when the investigator asks what the observations can identify, requests different sampling intervals, and tests competing explanations. The change is in how the problem is understood and investigated.

We built Cognitive Harness to make such changes part of an explicit learning process. The Harness organizes a foundation model's work around reusable cognitive patterns: strategies for posing questions, constructing explanations, changing representations, separating hypotheses, and revising concepts. It selects or composes a pattern, uses that pattern to organize reasoning, and checks the claims the reasoning produces.

The foundation model and the Harness together form the Cognizer system described in our technical report. The model proposes interpretations and candidate reasoning. The Harness maintains the demand, coordinates verification, schedules further investigations, and retains experience for later tasks. In the experiments reported here, the model weights remain fixed while the Harness adapts at test time.

The central mechanism is Test-time Recursive Harness Improvement (TRHI). It connects two feedback loops. Within a task, an unresolved claim or a concrete counterexample can create a narrower investigation. Its result returns to the original question for another check. Across tasks, admitted experience changes the evidence, pattern preferences, and strategies available to the next investigation.

Our report examines these mechanisms through three instrumented traces, comparisons on LiveBench Zebra, AIME 2025, and LiveCodeBench, component ablations, and a cross-episode learning experiment. The results connect the internal operation of the Harness to changes in answer quality, completion, and inference cost. Three observations organize the discussion:

  • Verification changes the work. A failed check can identify the precise condition that needs repair, create a narrower demand, and produce evidence for checking the original task again.
  • Cognitive patterns affect how problems are decomposed. Selecting a different pattern changes which claims are formed, which alternatives are examined, and which observations are requested.
  • Experience changes later investigations. On held-out Zebra tasks, persistent experience improves reported accuracy and reduces token use relative to resetting the library between episodes.

The Harness targets two values described in the report. The first is efficiency: earlier investigations can help the system avoid redundant search and concentrate resources on useful checks. The second is discovery: a discrepancy can motivate a deeper explanation, and a new abstraction can make a previously inaccessible question available for investigation. Cognitive patterns connect these aims to concrete changes in the work.

This also makes deployment experience useful at the level of strategy. Organizational procedures, design rules, failure records, codebases, and measurements can supply knowledge and candidate patterns. As the Harness takes on tasks, its checks add experience about which of these structures are productive and how they should be applied. The repertoire develops through the problems the system actually investigates.

The method follows four steps: observe and question, abstract, reason, and experiment and verify. Learning connects those steps across time. An investigation can finish with a checked result, an improved strategy for a recurring class of problems, and a record of the observations and conditions that made the result possible.

Method

The Harness begins with a demand: an objective, constraints, available information, and the checks required to resolve it. Each demand passes through the same four-step cycle. A child investigation runs its own cycle and returns to its parent; experience from completed checks can then inform later episodes.

Four objects make this process explicit: the demand anchor, the active cognitive pattern, the Chain of Logic, and the verification record. Together they connect what the model is trying to establish with how it reasons and how the result is assessed.

Observe and question

The demand anchor records the goal, constraints, obligations, and acceptance criteria. The Harness organizes surrounding information into three domains: established premises (K), obtainable but unobserved information (U), and open claims requiring a check (R). This partition determines what can support the investigation and what work remains.

An admissible observation can establish a premise. If a needed observation is unavailable, the corresponding obligation stays open. The anchor also records which sources, interactions, and checks are available. A child anchor preserves the original obligation while narrowing the question to the missing evidence or condition.

Abstract

Next Cognitive Pattern Prediction (NCPP) selects a reasoning strategy from the Cognitive Prior Library in light of the demand, evidence, open obligations, prior outcomes, and available budget. A single activation uses one pattern; a multi-pattern activation coordinates several while preserving their identities; composition abstracts a new candidate pattern and its concepts.

The report's consolidated library contains 67 patterns across eleven themes. Examples include premise examination, counterexample testing, symmetry and invariants, inverse problems, and identifiability. An entry describes its central idea, role in reasoning, worked example, and conditions of use. The selected pattern becomes the cognitive backbone of the next step.

Reason

The active backbone expands the demand into a Chain of Logic comprising minimal empirical logic units (MELUs). Each unit records a checkable relationship, its supporting material, dependencies, and open conditions. Direct observations, assumptions, and transfers from other domains are recorded separately.

Decomposition can identify a sub-demand before a check fails. Reflection can revise the decomposition after an objection, while extrapolation can propose a claim whose assumptions become testable obligations. The Chain of Logic provides a record that the verifier can inspect and revisit as the investigation develops.

Experiment and verify

Checks assess individual units and the assembled answer against the demand's requirements. They produce a verdict together with the basis of the check: model review, simulation, or an executed empirical check. The Harness uses that record to determine which obligations have been resolved and what evidence can be carried forward.

A concrete, actionable failure can initiate a bounded child investigation. The child returns its result for checking against the parent's original requirement, followed by a check of the complete answer. Depth, branching, revisions, and available computation govern the scheduler's next action.

Learning through the cycle

Across episodes, the Harness retains admitted results with their supporting claims, verification basis, and conditions of use. Eligible successes and failures update preferences for patterns under comparable checks. The repertoire can also include newly composed strategies, allowing later demands to draw on a different set of reasoning structures.

This is where the four-step cycle becomes a learning process. Pattern composition occurs during abstraction; reasoning applies the candidate; verification supplies feedback; retained experience changes later activation. The model weights remain fixed while context and explicit Harness state develop through experience.

Results

The experiments examine what changes when a model's reasoning is organized around explicit cognitive patterns, checkable claims, and recursive feedback. We study both timescales of the method: how a check changes the current investigation, and how retained experience changes a later one. Three instrumented traces show these processes in detail. Benchmark comparisons, ablations, and a persistence experiment measure their effects on task performance.

The reported benchmarks cover complementary forms of reasoning. LiveBench Zebra tests constraint reasoning, AIME 2025 tests exact mathematical answers, and LiveCodeBench tests executable programs. Final answers are graded against references or held-out tests. The comparisons include a bare model call, budgeted selection, Claude Code, and the full TRHI system within the supplied model blocks.

Accuracy, completion, and inference cost

The full Harness reports higher accuracy and fewer incorrect assertions than Claude Code in each model–benchmark block. The LiveCodeBench comparison below shows the differences for Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus. The largest improvement in this comparison appears in the Haiku block, where accuracy rises from 80.9% to 87.6%.

Accuracy and incorrect-assertion rate both use all valid runs as their denominator. Completion coverage counts the share returning a complete answer, whether correct or incorrect. Reading these measures together shows how often the system resolves a task and how reliable its completed answers are.

For Haiku on LiveCodeBench, the accuracy gain of 6.7 percentage points accompanies a reduction in incorrect assertions from 17.5% to 6.2%. Completion coverage changes from 98.4% to 93.8%. The Harness therefore returns more correct answers per valid run while leaving a larger fraction of tasks partially resolved.

The other model blocks follow the same direction. Sonnet's accuracy rises from 90.3% to 93.6%, with incorrect assertions falling from 8.8% to 3.2%. Opus moves from 93.4% to 95.0% accuracy and from 6.0% to 2.2% incorrect assertions. These measures reflect the combined operation of pattern activation, decomposition, and verification.

The system spends additional computation on that process. Across the reported benchmark comparisons, mean tokens increase from 281.4k to 329.7k for Haiku, from 244.6k to 270.8k for Sonnet, and from 218.3k to 230.4k for Opus. The report presents accuracy, incorrect assertions, completion, and token use together so that the outcome of the investigation can be read alongside the work required to produce it.

The traces explain where that work goes. A verifier may investigate a competing explanation, construct a counterexample, or check a repaired component against its original requirements. Those operations change the set of obligations that the Harness can resolve. The following examples show how this unfolds inside an episode.

A counterexample becomes a smaller investigation

The first trace isolates verification and recursion on a ceiling-division helper. The task requires a function for nonnegative numerators and positive divisors, with invalid divisors rejected. The initial implementation fails 419 of 3,030 test cases. One failing input is particularly direct: the function returns 1 for a zero numerator and unit divisor, where the expected answer is 0.

That counterexample becomes the open question of a child demand. The child repairs the helper under the narrower requirement, and the checker tests both the repaired unit and the child's assembled answer. The result then returns to the parent, where the helper is checked against the original task, including invalid-divisor cases outside the child's immediate scope.

The trace records five checks: initial rejection, repaired-unit verification, child-answer verification, parent recheck, and whole-answer verification. The rejected implementation is excluded from experience eligible for reuse. Each successful return is attached to the requirement it satisfied and the checks that established it.

The planner was scripted in this trace to isolate the verification and recursion machinery. The checker selected and executed its own tests. The sequence makes the role of the child investigation visible: it turns a specific objection into work that can be completed and then brought back to the original problem.

Refuting a branch changes the solution

The second trace compares TRHI with a Claude Code call of the same model on a five-position logic puzzle with 21 clues and a four-part graded answer. Both runs establish the same attributes at one position. Two remaining target cells depend on rejecting a branch whose contradiction appears only after several forced deductions.

TRHI separates the solution into four checked units. One unit assumes a candidate placement, propagates the consequences, and finds that two clues conflict. It returns the resulting exclusion to the rest of the solution. The later units then work with a smaller, consistent set of possibilities, and a final check tests the assembled grid against all 21 clues.

In the reported comparison, TRHI matches the reference answer. The Claude Code run expresses uncertainty about the two target cells and fills them incorrectly. Making the refutation a unit of its own allows the Harness to investigate the difficult branch directly and carry its consequence into the remaining reasoning.

A different pattern reveals a hidden current

The third trace follows an inverse problem in stochastic dynamics. A camera observes a fully observed linear diffusion at unit frame intervals. The recorded fluctuations appear consistent with equilibrium relaxation. The task asks whether the frames establish detailed balance and requires recovery of the underlying generator, stationary probability current, and entropy production.

The demand separates what is established from what can still be observed. Ordered cross-covariances, clocks, diffusion calibration, and an independent frequency bound are available as premises. Statistics at two additional sampling intervals can be requested. The equilibrium interpretation remains an open claim to check.

A principal-logarithm estimate fits the original frames and implies equilibrium. NCPP activates identifiability, inverse-problem reasoning, and severe testing to investigate what those frames can distinguish. This changes both the mathematical object being examined and the observations requested from the environment.

The investigation uses three child demands. Additional frame intervals distinguish possibilities that coincide at the original sampling rate. The parent checks the combined evidence against the original recovery task. A further child tests the frequency bound on which uniqueness depends by constructing an alternative generator that reproduces the observed settings when that bound is relaxed.

The result connects a revised question to a different experiment and then to recovery of the hidden current under explicit conditions. The operative cognitive move is to characterize identifiability before selecting an estimate. This is also a reusable strategy: a later inverse problem can ask which distinct systems remain observationally equivalent and which measurement would separate them.

Following a result through its checks

The ceiling-division trace gives a detailed view of the verification record. After the initial rejection, the repaired unit passes 200,432 checks. The child's whole answer then passes 20,720 checks. At the parent, the result is tested on 310,300 valid inputs and 20 invalid-divisor cases before the final whole-answer check runs 105,020 checks.

Those stages correspond to different questions. The unit check asks whether the repaired component meets its specification. The child check asks whether the narrower demand has been resolved. The parent check brings back the original requirements, and the root check assesses the complete answer. A returned result moves through those requirements with its execution record attached.

The same organization supports other forms of investigation. In a logic puzzle, the record can establish that a branch contradicts the clues. In an inverse problem, it can record the observations that distinguish competing generators. The Harness uses a common representation for the claim, its supporting work, and the requirement against which it was checked.

When a result is retained, the record preserves its verification basis. Subsequent pattern preferences are updated separately for comparable evidence. This connects the within-task sequence of checks to the cross-task learning experiment: the later investigation receives experience organized by how it was established and which problem it helped resolve.

What the component ablations change

The ablations remove one Harness component at a time while keeping the model, tools, admission threshold, and budget of the full system. The tested components are Next Cognitive Pattern Prediction, each part of the K/U/R information partition, and sub-demand anchors. Removing sub-demand anchors also removes within-task recursion.

Every ablation lowers LiveCodeBench accuracy in all three model blocks. The way performance changes differs by component. The Haiku results below make those differences concrete: some removals increase incorrect assertions, while others leave more work unresolved.

Removing open obligations R raises Haiku's incorrect-assertion rate from 6.2% to 13.7%, while partial returns fall from 6.2% to 2.7%. In this configuration, model proposals can enter the anchor as premises. The system completes more claims without preserving the same distinction between what is established and what still requires verification.

Removing established premises K produces a related profile at a smaller magnitude. Haiku's LiveCodeBench accuracy falls to 84.6%, and incorrect assertions rise to 10.7%. K and R work together to organize what can support a claim and which obligations remain to be discharged.

Removing unobserved information U lowers Haiku's accuracy to 86.8%. Obligations requiring additional information remain open when that information is no longer represented as something the system can request. The report observes lower LiveCodeBench accuracy in every model block under this ablation.

Removing sub-demands reduces Haiku's accuracy from 87.6% to 82.9%. Partial returns rise to 9.7%. Without a narrower anchor, the system loses a way to isolate an unresolved condition and return a checked result to the parent. More of the original problem remains unfinished.

Removing NCPP also reduces accuracy, to 84.8% in the Haiku comparison, and increases token use by 6.5%. The Chain of Logic still exists, but it no longer receives the same explicit cognitive backbone for organizing the investigation. Pattern selection influences how the system spends its reasoning and checking effort.

Together, the ablations distinguish two contributions. K and R support the reliability of completed answers. U, sub-demand anchors, and pattern prediction help the Harness resolve more obligations. The full system combines these functions: it organizes the information available to the investigator, chooses a strategy, and creates additional work when a requirement needs a more focused treatment.

What changes across episodes

The persistence experiment asks whether experience from earlier tasks improves later investigation. It compares a persistent Harness with one reset to its initial Cognitive Prior Library between episodes. Development episodes build experience, and held-out evaluation measures what transfers. The foundation model remains fixed throughout the comparison.

For Haiku on Zebra, persistent experience raises reported held-out accuracy from 92.0% to 95.1%. Mean held-out tokens per task fall from 311.6k to 242.8k. The retained state therefore changes both the success of the later investigation and the work it requires.

These outcomes reflect more than retrieving an earlier answer. The retained record can affect which premises are available, which patterns are preferred, and how a new demand is decomposed. A pattern that worked under a relevant class of checks can receive greater selection weight, directing a later investigation toward a productive strategy earlier.

Within the persistent Haiku run, revisions per verified node fall from 1.45 early to 0.94 late, alongside rising verification coverage. This provides a view of how execution changes as experience accumulates: more nodes reach verification while verified nodes require fewer revisions. The held-out token figures exclude the cost of building that experience.

The report also compares how the system organizes the evidence behind reuse. Pooling pattern weights across verification bases increases reported reuse but produces more incorrect assertions than basis-specific weighting in all three model blocks. Keeping those records separate preserves the relationship between a pattern's preference and the kind of check that supported its earlier use.

A no-composition condition retains part of the improvement over the reset library. Existing patterns can therefore become more useful through experience-driven selection and reweighting. Composition provides an additional way for the repertoire to change by proposing a strategy that the current library does not adequately supply.

In the system's design, composition and admission occupy different moments of the cycle. Step 2 creates the candidate pattern and its concepts. Steps 3 and 4 apply it and check the resulting claims. The admitted outcome can then change its later selection weight. The experience loop connects a proposed strategy to the work it enables and the evidence produced by that work.

The pattern library as a record of inquiry

The consolidated Cognitive Prior Library contains 67 patterns organized into eleven themes. The entries span concept formation, mathematical structures, explanation, inference, experimental design, and research methodology. Each gives a central idea, a role in reasoning, a worked example, and conditions for use.

The patterns describe concrete changes an investigator can make. A dual formulation can turn a bound on one class of objects into information about another. Mechanistic explanation asks what an intervention would change. Coarse-graining examines how an omitted variable can affect a visible process through memory. Identifiability analysis asks which mechanisms remain indistinguishable under the available observations.

NCPP makes these strategies available at the point where a demand needs a cognitive backbone. Multiple patterns can coordinate complementary roles, and a child demand can activate a different backbone when its question changes. The reusable object is the move that organizes inquiry, together with the evidence that informs when to use it.

The results connect this repertoire to two forms of improvement. Recursive checking refines the current answer, while persistent experience changes the starting conditions of a later task. The traces show how those mechanisms operate; the benchmark and ablation comparisons measure their effect within an episode; the persistence study measures their effect across episodes. Looking forward

The current system studies learning through context and explicit Harness state. The next phases extend the same organizing idea: cognitive patterns can become objects that a model learns to activate, compose, and improve through experience. The technical report develops this direction across post-training, pretraining, and interaction with the physical world.

Internalizing verified patterns

The post-training phase would study whether selected, verified patterns can be internalized in model weights. Experience from the Harness supplies records of the question, the strategy used, the claims it generated, and the checks that followed. Those records connect a reasoning pattern to an observable role in solving a problem.

The explicit library would remain useful alongside trained capabilities. It provides a place to inspect a pattern, adapt it to local procedures, record its conditions, and incorporate new experience at deployment. A model could bring an internalized repertoire to a task while the Harness continues to organize external evidence and task-specific adaptation.

This creates a relationship between two timescales of learning. Test-time adaptation can discover which structures are productive in a particular setting. Training can study how selected experience changes the model's ability to recognize and apply those structures. The report places the present experiments in the test-time phase of that roadmap.

Predicting cognitive patterns during pretraining

A longer-term direction is to investigate cognitive pattern prediction as a pretraining objective. NCPP currently operates over explicit textual entries placed in the model's context. The broader research question is how a model can learn representations of the strategies that organize inquiry and predict which strategy a developing problem calls for.

A pattern can connect observations to a new question, map a relation across domains, or introduce a representation in which a difficult dependency becomes tractable. Learning to identify and compose those structures would bring the object of adaptation closer to the mechanisms that generate a solution procedure.

The report's six-level taxonomy organizes this idea around the locus of adaptation: the highest-level object that experience can change. The transition studied by the Harness concerns changes to the structures that generate procedures. Cognitive pattern prediction gives that transition an explicit learning target.

Connecting digital and physical investigations

The physical-world direction connects computational exploration with observation and intervention. Physical experience can supply candidate mechanisms, constraints, and discrepancies. Digital investigations can explore their consequences and develop candidate strategies. The resulting records can then guide the next observation or intervention.

For example, a discrepancy between a model and a measurement can motivate a new abstraction or a targeted experiment. The Harness would organize the observations, assumptions, and open claims, select a pattern for investigating them, and retain the results of the ensuing checks. Each pass through the loop would connect a proposed explanation to an action and its measured consequences.

The report also proposes studying how checked results travel between separate Harnesses. A recipient can reassess the result under its own acceptance standard using the recorded evidence and conditions. This would connect local learning with the exchange of reusable cognitive experience.

Across these directions, the central object remains the same: a cognitive pattern that changes what an investigator can ask, infer, or test. The roadmap extends the channels through which those patterns are learned and the environments in which their consequences can be examined.

Conclusions

Cognitive Harness makes reasoning patterns available for explicit selection, composition, verification, and revision at test time. The foundation model proposes the reasoning; the Harness organizes it into demands, cognitive backbones, checkable claims, and evidence-linked outcomes. The four-step cycle provides a common structure for that work.

Its two feedback paths connect refinement with learning. Within an episode, an unresolved obligation can become a narrower demand. The child result returns to the parent for checking against the original objective. Across episodes, admitted experience updates the records and pattern preferences available to later investigations.

The instrumented traces show these mechanisms in action. A concrete counterexample leads to a repaired helper and a parent recheck. A logic-puzzle branch becomes an independent refutation whose result guides the remaining solution. An inverse problem changes through identifiability analysis, additional observations, and a test of the conditions supporting recovery.

The quantitative comparisons examine the same system at the task level. The full Harness reports higher accuracy and fewer incorrect assertions across the supplied model–benchmark blocks. Component ablations show how information partitioning, cognitive pattern prediction, and recursive sub-demands contribute to those outcomes. The persistence experiment shows improved held-out accuracy and lower held-out token use when experience is retained.

These results distinguish the roles of the system's components. Explicit premises and open obligations help organize reliable conclusions. Unobserved information, pattern selection, and sub-demands help the system pursue the work needed to resolve more of the task. Retained experience changes which reasoning structures a subsequent episode can activate.

The Cognitive Prior Library gives this adaptation a reusable form. Its entries record strategies for changing a question, constructing an explanation, selecting an experiment, or revising a concept. The active strategy shapes the Chain of Logic, while verification connects that strategy to the claims and outcomes it produces.

The current phase develops these mechanisms with fixed foundation-model weights. Context and Harness state carry the adaptation. The roadmap extends the work toward post-training, cognitive pattern prediction during pretraining, and investigations that connect digital reasoning with physical observation.

The design also gives experience a concrete place in deployment. An organization's procedures and measurements can supply the starting knowledge for an investigation. Its subsequent checks add records of successes, failures, and useful changes of strategy. The explicit library connects that local experience to future reasoning while keeping the patterns available for inspection and adaptation.

A new question can therefore begin with more than an earlier answer. It can begin with a different preference over patterns, an applicable result, or a newly available way of decomposing the task. This is the connection between the revised pattern in the concept illustration and the later investigation that calls on it.

The broader direction is a system that can learn how to investigate. A useful result can resolve the current demand and also change the strategies available to the next one. Cognitive Harness provides an architecture for making that progression explicit: observe and question, abstract, reason, experiment and verify, then carry qualified experience forward.

Appendix

Terminology and report guide

Demand anchor
The objective, constraints, information domain, obligations, and required checks for an investigation.
NCPP
Next Cognitive Pattern Prediction: selection, coordination, or composition of the strategy that guides reasoning.
Chain of Logic
Checkable units and the dependencies among their claims, evidence, assumptions, and open conditions.
Inner / outer recursion
Further investigation within the current episode, and retention of admitted experience for later episodes.
